recos for building

  1. pull from where work already happens, so nobody changes how they work. i used slack, linear, notion and meeting notes from granola or fireflies.
  2. filter hard. only what crosses teams, only what changed since the last digest, and up to 8 items of 15 words each with an owner and a link.
  3. let it read its own last post. that's how it knows what changed. any scheduler with an ai step can do this. i used zapier. make or n8n work too.
  4. test it in a private channel first. a few runs a day for two days is enough to get the output right before it reaches the team.

hiring faster

what i think works best today, after ~30 remote hires end to end + screening hundreds of applicants: a short form and an automated back end so one person can manage volume

the front end

keep the form short. strong candidates can communicate concisely, without the long, over-structured application.

the back end: how i'd get from 200 applicants to a shortlist of ~6 in a day

  1. ai cuts the hard dealbreakers. a no on the non-negotiable, wrong timezone or language, dead or missing links, zero-effort answers. 200 → ~65-70.
  2. ai sorts the rest into two buckets: direct role matches, and "outlier spikes" (people who built something obsessive from scratch, hacked growth, or put up real numbers in an unrelated field). the unusual ones don't get averaged out.
  3. go through all ~65-70 manually, proof-of-work link first, cv second. this keeps the ~25-30 with real ownership.
  4. a research bot verifies the ~25-30, checking whether the project has real traction, whether their linkedin, x and github match what they say they owned, and how they write and show up online. it runs last so it confirms claims without filtering out outliers with a thin footprint. it emails me a summary of each for easy scheduling, and i pick ~6 to schedule intro calls with.

promoting from within + lower costs

talent spotting on your own team comes before hiring. i got four people already on our team into open roles, which saved $150k+ a year against outside hires at $80-120k each, after raises and junior backfills.

who i look for

some of your heaviest contributors can be your quietest people, and no dashboard shows them. i pay attention to who's carrying more than their title and who's ready for more.

worth asking before you post a role: who's already doing part of the job?

never missing a deadline

100+ ads across markets, each with its own rules, budget and air date. none went out late.

protecting a deadline

count back from the date.every earlier deadline gets set from it.
start the slowest sign-off first.legal, a partner or one stakeholder usually sets the real timeline.
set a last day for changes, and hold it.late changes are what make dates slip.
give every piece one owner.hand it out, then track everything in one place.

worth asking before your next launch: what's the last day anything can change?
